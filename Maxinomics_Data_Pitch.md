# Data Was Never "The New Oil" — Until Now

*Maxinomics · video pitch · draft v2*

**Target format:** ~18–22 minute longform documentary, with companion Shorts

---

> Everyone says data is the new oil. The metaphor is backwards — and the backwardness is the story. Oil gets burned; data gets copied, so it should be the one resource we can never run out of. Yet AI is hitting a wall. This is the story of how that happened, why we've heard this exact alarm before (about oil, for a century), and the one question that decides whether the wall is real: is synthetic data the fracking that defers it forever, or the trap where the resource feeds on itself and rots?

## The hook

Your entire digital self sells for about 36 cents. The industry built on selling it is worth more than a quarter-trillion dollars. And for 150 years, experts kept predicting we'd run out of oil — and were wrong nearly every time. Now they're saying it about data. The film opens on that collision: how can the most copyable resource ever created suddenly be running out?

## The thesis

"Data is the new oil" gets the metaphor backwards, and the backwardness is the whole story. Oil is consumed — burn a barrel and it's gone. Data is copied — use a dataset and it's still there, ready for the next thousand models. By its nature, data is the one resource you should never be able to exhaust, and that non-rival quality is exactly why the digital economy worked: you could harvest human behavior endlessly without ever using it up.

And yet the labs are hitting a wall — a strange one. Not a depleted stock, but an exhausted flow: the machines have already eaten essentially the entire library of human writing, and we don't produce new high-quality text fast enough to feed the next model. But we've heard "we're running out" before — about oil, for a century — and a new extraction technology made fools of the prophets every time (most recently fracking). Data's fracking is synthetic data: machines manufacturing their own fuel. Which leaves one question for the film to answer.

**The question:** *not "will AI run out of data," but "can you run out of something you never use up — and does the machine that refills the tank end up poisoning it?"*

## The story — seven chapters

1. **The byproduct nobody wanted.** Humans have collected data for centuries — censuses, John Graunt predicting plague from London mortality tables in the 1660s — but it was a tool, never a tradable asset. The data market is young: credit bureaus, mailing lists, then the loyalty-card revolution (Tesco's Clubcard, 1995). Plant the metaphor here — "data is the new oil" (2006) — as the idea the film will test.

2. **Why data isn't oil at all.** The conceptual turn most takes skip. Oil is rival and consumed; data is non-rival and copied — using it doesn't use it up, and one dataset can train a thousand models. By this logic, running out of data should be impossible. Hold that contradiction; the rest of the film detonates it.

3. **The exhaust economy.** How copyable "exhaust" became the most valuable business model on earth. The brokers quantify you (Acxiom: ~2.5B people, 12,000+ attributes); recommendation engines turn data into behavior (~35% of Amazon sales, ~80% of Netflix viewing, ~70% of YouTube watch time); the whole ~$1 trillion ad economy rides on it. Data as steering wheel.

4. **The machines that eat data.** LLMs are the most data-hungry things ever built. The modern-AI bet in one line: more data + more compute = more capability. Data's role shifts from "describes you" to "builds the mind" — and the appetite sets up the wall.

5. **The strange wall.** Epoch AI projects the usable stock of human text is fully consumed between 2026 and 2032 (central ~2028). But you don't consume data — so this isn't depletion, it's a flow problem: we've ingested the whole library and humans don't write new high-quality text fast enough to feed the next model. A scarcity oil never had.

6. **We've seen this movie.** Pay off the cold open. Oil's century of false alarms — Hubbert's 1956 forecast nailed the 1970 US peak, then shale blew past it after 2009. Every wall was deferred by new extraction. Data's fracking is synthetic data (Gartner projects it overtakes real data in AI training by 2030). Follow the new money: the licensing scramble (News Corp $250M+, Reddit $60M/yr) and the synthetic-data boom.

7. **Does the streak break?** The climax is a genuine open question. Data may be even more deferrable than oil — you can manufacture it. Except for the one failure mode oil never faced: model collapse, where a resource fed on its own copies quietly degrades. The honest landing — the era of free, infinite data is over, and the scramble (the money) is the tell, whichever way it breaks.

## The original analysis (the data spine)

One original cut from public data, the way every Maxinomics longform carries one.

- **The crossover (hero visual).** Built from Epoch AI's published dataset — human-text stock vs. frontier-model training size, with the 2026–2032 window shaded and ~2028 marked. The climax frame, on first-party numbers.
- **The deferral overlay.** On the same chart, add the "fracking" line — effective data once synthetic generation is included — to show the wall sliding right. It visualizes the open question directly.
- **Bonus dataset: the broker census.** Scrape and merge the four public state data-broker registries (California/CPPA, Vermont, Texas, Oregon) into the most complete public picture of the industry — count, map, and flag who collects location and minors' data.
