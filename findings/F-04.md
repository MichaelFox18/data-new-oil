# F-04  The CCPA request flood is automated and top-heavy — and ~99% fulfilled

CLAIM (one sentence, specific, falsifiable):
  Across the 581 California-registered data brokers' 2024 filings, reported consumer
  privacy requests are dominated by automated signals and a few firms — the 10 busiest
  brokers account for 67.7% of all reported request-events and one broker (Cuebiq) logs a
  near-identical ~26.3M under three different request types — while brokers report
  fulfilling ~99% of them, at a median response of 3-6 days.

TYPE:        MEASURED (with self-report caveats; see ALTERNATIVES)
NUMBERS:     Per request type (received | compliance | brokers reporting >0 | median days):
             - opt out of sale/sharing .... 98,671,954 | 98.3% | 401 | 3
             - delete .................... 57,959,768 | 98.6% | 430 | 5.5
             - limit sensitive PI ........ 30,951,629 | 99.6% | 127 | 3
             - know (collected) ..........  4,310,186 | ~100% | 336 | 5
             - know (sold/shared) ........  2,831,882 | ~101% | 275 | 6
             Sum of request-EVENTS across the 5 types: 194,725,419 received /
             192,124,403 complied (98.7%) / 2,790,305 denied. NOTE this sum double/triple-
             counts automated signals and is NOT a count of distinct requests.
             Concentration: top-10 brokers = 67.7% of request-events; 131 of 581 brokers

             opt-out 26,284,478 / limit 26,284,478 — near-identical), Eyeota (14.70M),

CONFIDENCE:  Medium. Values verified against the raw CSV (Cuebiq matched exactly), and the
             compliance/concentration patterns are robust — but the inputs are
             self-reported and internally inconsistent (see ALTERNATIVES), so the absolute
             totals are soft and must be framed as "reported request-events", not people.
NOVELTY:     LOW — CORRECTED by prior-art check (2026-06-04). This is well-trodden:
             Bloomberg Law ("Most California Data Brokers Saw Few Privacy Opt-Out
             Requests") reported the request distribution (25th/median/75th = 221/3,268/
             63,170; max ~29.6M; ~150 brokers with zero deletion requests) and Cuebiq's
             ~26M deletion figure; an arXiv paper ("Privacy Without Remedy: An Assessment
             of Data Broker Compliance with California Privacy Law", arXiv:2605.21376)
             assesses broker compliance directly. So volumes / compliance / "Cuebiq is the
             outlier" are NOT novel — F-04 is supporting context + a validity cross-check,
             not a discovery. The one possibly-original sliver: the SAME ~26.28M under
             THREE request types (delete/opt-out/limit) as an automated-signal fingerprint,
             which the deletion-focused coverage may not have drawn out. RECOMMEND: treat
             as supporting context/triangulation (demote), keep only the automation tell as
             a candidate original cut.
ALTERNATIVES / confounders:
             - AUTOMATION: identical counts across delete/opt-out/limit (Cuebiq) indicate
               machine signals (Global Privacy Control, authorized-agent bulk) counted in
               several categories — so the cross-type sum is NOT 195M individual people.
             - Self-report noise: two request types show compliance >100% (complied >
               received), proving the fields aren't internally reconciled.
             - Concentration may reflect business model (large-audience location/identity
               brokers) rather than reporting error.
             - "Received" definitions likely vary by broker (some count GPC, some don't).
