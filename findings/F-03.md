# F-03  Language-model training sets grew ~5x/year to 36 trillion tokens

CLAIM (one sentence, specific, falsifiable):
  In the modern scaling era (2018-2026), the training-set size of language models grew
  about 5x per year (2.35 doublings/yr; ~1.4 years per 10x), reaching a maximum of 36
  trillion tokens (Qwen3-Max, Sep 2025) — putting frontier training within roughly one
  order of magnitude of Epoch's REPORTED ~10^14-token stock of usable human text.

TYPE:        MEASURED (growth + max). The human-text-stock comparison is REPORTED context
             (Epoch), not measured here.
NUMBERS:     Domain = Language, training size in tokens:
             - 2018+ (modern): n=212, 5.1x/yr, 2.35 doublings/yr, 1.41 yr per 10x,
               R^2 = 0.46, window 2018-01-30 .. 2026-04-27
             - full 1993+: n=279, 1.89x/yr, 0.92 doublings/yr, R^2 = 0.35
             - max training set: 3.6e13 tokens (Qwen3-Max, 2025-09-05); recent frontier
               cluster ~20-36T (Qwen3-Max, GLM-4.5/4.6, Ling-1T)
SOURCES:     SRC-001 Epoch notable_ai_models.csv (Tier A, CC-BY). Single authoritative
             source; the originating group's own dataset.
METHOD:      src/analyze/epoch_growth.py — filter Domain=="Language"; tokens = "Training
             dataset size (total)"; OLS of log10(tokens) on decimal publication year;
             report two windows. Output: results/epoch_growth.json.
COVERAGE:    279 of 385 language models carry both tokens + date (~35% missing size).
             Excludes multimodal models. This is the POPULATION of notable language
             models, not a frontier-only series.
CONFIDENCE:  Medium. Sign and order of the rate are robust, but R^2 = 0.46 reflects real
             scatter (many non-frontier models at any date). A frontier-only fit would
             sharpen it.
NOVELTY:     Supporting/spine, not a novel discovery: Epoch itself publishes growth
             analyses. The value here is recomputing the rate from first-party data to
             build the hero crossover chart honestly, rather than screenshotting it.
             (PRIOR-ART: Epoch's own scaling posts — cite alongside.)
ALTERNATIVES / confounders:
             - Population vs. frontier: the aggregate understates the frontier's scaling;
               present as a population trend, and add a frontier-only (max-per-period) cut.
             - Missing-size models (~35%) could bias if missingness correlates with size.
             - Token-counting definitions vary across labs/notes; unit is tokens for
               Language domain but quality/epochs differ.
             - "Notable models" = a curation (survivorship) choice by Epoch.
ROBUSTNESS:  Two time windows reported; deterministic re-run; planned cross-checks:

             2018 cutoff.
